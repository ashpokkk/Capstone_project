**Question:** What income tax rate applies to someone earning Rs. 2500000 a year?

**Expected behavior:** Should retrieve Section 149 bracket and compute correctly

**Answer:**

**Tax = Rs 116,000 + 20 % × ( taxable income – Rs 2,200,000 )**

- Excess over Rs 2,200,000 = Rs 300,000

## Q17 (Income Tax Basics / direct)

**Question:** Who counts as a tax resident of Pakistan?

**Expected behavior:** Should retrieve and answer from corpus

**Answer:**

A tax resident for a tax year in Pakistan is determined as follows:

**Individuals** – An individual is a resident if **any** of the three conditions below is met【1】  
1. The person is present in Pakistan for a total of **183 days or more** in that tax year.  
2. The person is present in Pakistan for **120 days or more** in that tax year **and** has been present in Pakistan for at least **365 days in total during the four preceding tax years**.  
3. The person is an **employee or official of the Federal Government or a Provincial Government who is posted abroad** for the tax year.

**Associations of Persons (AOP)** – An AOP is a resident when the **control and management of its affairs is situated wholly or partly in Pakistan at any time during the year**【1】.

**Companies** – A company is a resident when any of the following is true【1】

## Q18 (Income Tax Basics / direct)

**Question:** What is a Tax Year vs a Special Tax Year?

**Expected behavior:** Should retrieve and answer from corpus

**Answer:**

A **Tax Year** (also called the Normal Tax Year) is the regular twelve‑month financial period that ends on 30 June. It is identified by the calendar year in which that 30 June falls. For example, the period 1 July 2017 to 30 June 2018 is the tax year denoted as calendar year 2018, and the period 1 July 2018 to 30 June 2019 is denoted as calendar year 2019【2】.  

A **Special Tax Year** is also a twelve‑month period, but it is defined by any twelve‑month span whose closing date falls in a different calendar year than the normal financial year. It is denoted by the calendar year in which its closing date occurs. For example, a period 1 January 2017 to 31 December 2017 is a special tax year denoted as calendar year 2018, and a period 1 October 2017 to 30 September 2018 is a special tax year denoted as calendar year 2019【1】.  

Both concepts refer to twelve‑month periods; the distinction is that the normal tax year always ends on 30 June, whereas a special tax year can end on any date and is labeled by the year in which that end date falls.
